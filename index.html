Why this matters

The Challenge

Global health is entering a rapidly changing era. Artificial intelligence is transforming how information is produced and consumed, misinformation can spread rapidly, and global health programs are operating in an increasingly complex funding and policy environment.


In this context, producing good evidence is no longer enough. We also need to become much better at communicating why that evidence matters.

The future we want

Our Vision

A future in which researchers do not simply publish evidence, but learn to communicate it; where communities are informed participants in decisions affecting their health; and where the public can understand, question, and engage with the evidence shaping health decisions.

Evidence should travel further.

From research findings to real-world understanding, public dialogue, and more informed decisions.

Evidence deserves a public life

A Stronger Bridge for Global Health

When global health programs face changing priorities and funding pressures, people need to understand what investments accomplish. We should show not only the numbers, but the people behind them, the outcomes achieved, the limitations that remain, and the consequences of different choices.


Better communication can contribute to a more informed public conversation about global health while maintaining scientific integrity.

“We should not leave science inside the paper.”

We should build the skills, partnerships, and platforms needed to take it into communities, conversations, and sustainable action.
