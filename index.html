Science Beyond the Paper

Building a stronger bridge between global health evidence and the public.

Global health is entering a rapidly changing era. Artificial intelligence is transforming how information is produced and consumed, misinformation can spread rapidly, and global health programs are operating in an increasingly complex funding and policy environment. In this context, producing good evidence is no longer enough. We also need to become much better at communicating why that evidence matters.

My idea is to build Science Beyond the Paper as an interdisciplinary science communication and public engagement initiative that brings researchers together with filmmakers, journalists, designers, educators, technology professionals, policymakers, and communities. Together, we would transform important global health research into documentaries, visual stories, digital content, and educational resources that make evidence accessible without compromising scientific rigor.

More than a project — a movement

🧠 Researchers

Develop the skills to communicate work beyond academic audiences — without losing nuance or accuracy.

🌍 Communities

Gain greater opportunities to engage with evidence affecting their health, becoming informed participants rather than passive recipients.

📋 Policymakers

Access research in more accessible and actionable formats, bridging the gap between evidence and decisions.

🎬 Communicators

Work alongside scientists to tell complex stories responsibly — filmmakers, journalists, designers, and tech professionals.

🤝 Partnerships

Universities, health organizations, media, tech companies, and communities build shared global health challenges.

📣 Advocacy

Show what investments in global health actually accomplish — the evidence, the people, the outcomes, and the choices.

Advocacy is part of the story

When global health programs face changing priorities and funding pressures, people need to understand what investments in global health actually accomplish. A health intervention should not be represented only as a budget line or a statistic.

We should be able to show the evidence, the people behind the numbers, the outcomes achieved, the limitations that remain, and the consequences of different choices. Better communication can contribute to a more informed public conversation about global health while maintaining scientific integrity.

“We should not leave science inside the paper. We should build the skills, partnerships, and platforms needed to take it into communities, conversations, and sustainable actions.”

My vision

A future in which researchers do not simply publish evidence, but learn to communicate it; where communities are not simply recipients of global health interventions, but informed participants; and where the public can understand, question, and engage with the evidence shaping health decisions.

The future of global health will depend not only on our ability to generate new evidence and develop revolutionary technologies, but also on our ability to connect that knowledge with people.

“We should not leave science inside the paper.”

We should build the skills, partnerships, and platforms needed to take it into communities, conversations, and sustainable actions.
